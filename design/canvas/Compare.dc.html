3. Recommendation: D is the floor, C is the instrument, A is the encore

Build D first: it repairs exactly what was measured (position, missing cross-references, no time, leads nowhere) with the code and tokens that exist, and it is the only direction that owns "what is new" and "where does this source touch". Build C second as the room every object star opens into: it owns the two questions D cannot (what touches Table 18, who subscribes) and its one-hop version is cheap enough to sit on all 17,000 pages. Their "yes" cells barely overlap: together five of eight questions are a yes, and the other three (BE in Finance, version change, coverage gaps) are a "partly" with a lens in D and an exit to the instrument that owns them. A then rides on both: its code plane is D's namespace positions, its lines are C's link data, so Tilt becomes a system-level view later without new pipeline work. E is not a home but it is the exit dock inside D. B is a beautiful answer to one question, at the price of WebGL. F is the baseline and loses on every row.
