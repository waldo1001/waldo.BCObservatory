# Testing GitHub Copilot Knowledge Engineering: What Actually Works and What Doesn\’t

[Read the post](https://jeremyvyska.com/2025/09/09/testing-github-copilot-knowledge-engineering-what-actually-works-and-what-doesnt/) · Jeremy Vyska (Jeremy Vyska, MVP) · 2025-09-09 · 2118 words · tier community · reviewed (checked by Opus)

> GitHub Copilot's effectiveness for Business Central development improves significantly with domain-specific knowledge engineering, but the approach matters more than the amount of knowledge. A five-tier testing framework comparing generic programming knowledge, community documentation, atomic knowledge patterns, and Model Context Protocol delivery showed that systematic, context-aware knowledge delivery achieved 100x+ performance improvements consistently, while generic knowledge actually hurt optimization decisions.

## Key points

- Generic programming knowledge made Copilot suggestions worse for Business Central by creating confusion with domain-specific patterns
- Atomic, focused knowledge delivered systematically (Tier 4 with MCP) achieved 100x+ performance improvements in six of nine modules, far outperforming static documentation approaches
- Knowledge engineering effectiveness depends more on structured delivery and specificity than total knowledge volume
- AI assistants need knowledge about when NOT to optimize as much as when to optimize, requiring false-positive testing

## Quotes

- "Tier 1 (Generic Programming) was worse than no knowledge at all. About half the optimizations actually hurt performance." (Demonstrates that incorrect knowledge can actively harm AI decision-making, validating the need for domain-specific approaches)
- "The business intelligence module went from 64 seconds to 81 milliseconds - a 746x improvement." (Shows the magnitude of performance improvements possible with proper atomic knowledge engineering and systematic delivery)
