# SaaS ERP misconceptions: the “on-premises thinking trap”

[Read the post](https://demiliani.com/2026/01/20/saas-erp-misconceptions-the-on-premises-thinking-trap/) · Stefano Demiliani (Stefano Demiliani, MVP) · 2026-01-20 · 935 words · tier community · reviewed (checked by Opus)

> SaaS ERP deployments often fail when organizations apply on-premises thinking instead of cloud-native practices. The post explains how treating Business Central as a managed service with proper integration patterns, role shifts, and customization discipline delivers value, while recreating on-premises operational models creates unnecessary complexity and cost.

## Key points

- SaaS misconception: treating cloud ERP as on-premises software deployed elsewhere ignores architectural and operational differences
- Upgrade-safe layering in Business Central means extensions survive updates without rework, enabling agility through constraints not limitations
- Common deployment errors include centralizing job queue tasks, using polling instead of events, ad-hoc integrations without standards, and test data bloat in production
- Best practices: start with accept-as-is default, shift IT roles from infrastructure management to adoption and value, redesign integrations using APIs and events instead of database connections

## Quotes

- "SaaS is simply on-premises software deployed elsewhere. This fundamentally misses what makes SaaS architecturally and operationally distinct." (Identifies the core misconception that drives incorrect deployment and operational decisions in SaaS ERP projects)
- "SaaS platforms like Business Central deliver maximum value when treated as continuous, managed services. Applying on-premises rigor creates the worst of both worlds." (Clarifies the paradox where SaaS pricing with on-premises operations yields poor outcomes and explains why operational model alignment matters)
