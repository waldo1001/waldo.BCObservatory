# Directions EMEA 2026, Day 2: Cowork, Agentic Development and Fabric

[Read the post](https://thinkaboutit.be/2026/10/directions-emea-2026-day-2-cowork-agentic-development-and-fabric/) · think about IT - Steven Renders (Steven Renders) · 2026-10-29 · 1048 words · tier community · reviewed (checked by Opus)

> Directions EMEA 2026 Day 2 covered AI orchestration across applications through Cowork, agentic AL development with BCQuality, native Business Central-Fabric integration, and platform performance improvements. The post explains how Business Central is evolving from individual Copilot features to broader multi-agent workflows with architectural implications for partners.

## Key points

- Cowork orchestrates business processes across Microsoft 365 and Business Central, with agents handling ERP operations while maintaining permissions and transaction integrity
- AI-assisted AL development is shifting from code generation to managing development workflows, supported by BCQuality for Business Central-specific knowledge and BC-Bench for agent evaluation
- Native Business Central-Fabric mirroring replicates ERP data without consuming Business Central APIs, but requires proper data modeling and security for analytics and agents
- Platform improvements include table-extension storage optimization, AL-based index management, and Business Central Optimum collaboration for real-world performance
- Partners face new architectural questions about multi-agent processes: ownership, approvals, failure handling, monitoring, and maintaining business rules enforcement across distributed workflows

## Quotes

- "Business Central must remain responsible for enforcing business rules, permissions, and transaction integrity, regardless of which agent initiates an operation." (Establishes that multi-agent workflows don't reduce Business Central's core responsibility for data integrity and security, a critical architectural principle for distributed systems.)
- "Generating code is becoming easier. Demonstrating that an agent can solve realistic Business Central development tasks correctly is a different challenge." (Shows that AI-assisted development's real value lies not in code generation but in managing complete, validated development processes that meet real-world requirements.)
- "Access to data is only part of the problem. Agents also need to understand what that data represents and how it should be interpreted." (Highlights that data availability alone doesn't enable agent functionality; proper data semantics and modeling are essential for agents to make correct decisions.)
