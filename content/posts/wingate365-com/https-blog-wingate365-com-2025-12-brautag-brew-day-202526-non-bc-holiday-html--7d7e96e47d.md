> This is a non-Business Central post about a traditional Bavarian home brewing day, documenting the step-by-step process of brewing a Märzen beer including mashing, sparging, boiling, cooling, and fermentation with detailed explanations of brewing chemistry and techniques.

## Key points

- Multi-step mashing at different temperatures extracts different types of sugars to balance fermentability, body, and flavor in the final beer
- Different hop varieties are used for different purposes: noble hops for aroma and traditional character, high-alpha hops for efficient bittering
- A 90-minute boil sterilizes the wort, concentrates volume, extracts hop bitterness, removes off-flavors, and clarifies through protein coagulation
- Post-boil cooling can be done rapidly or slowly overnight depending on equipment and water conservation priorities before yeast pitching
- Fermentation takes 6-10 weeks, after which the finished beer is kegged with CO2 for carbonation and serving

## Quotes

- "By using multiple temperature steps, you control the balance between fermentable sugars (higher alcohol, drier finish) vs non-fermentable sugars (body, sweetness, mouthfeel)." (Explains the core principle of mashing that determines the final beer's character and quality)
