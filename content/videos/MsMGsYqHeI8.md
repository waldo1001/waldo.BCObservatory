# Episode 514: Do We Need AI to Fight AI? The Content Overload Nobody's Talking About

> Podcast discussion (Dynamics Corner, episode 514) on AI-generated content in the Business Central community: repeating AI writing patterns, reader trust, inaccurate technical examples, author responsibility, and the question of whether readers need AI to summarize and check other AI content.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MsMGsYqHeI8) · Dynamics Corner podcast · 2026-04-21 · 52:56 · tier community · reviewed (checked by Opus)

## Overview

Hosts and guest Natalie Carillac discuss content overload and how AI-generated blog posts about Business Central change how readers judge sources. They describe patterns that suggest AI authorship, such as bold text, bullet points, conclusions, dashes and emojis, and note that this detection is not reliable and human writing can match the same patterns.

They separate AI-created content (generated and published as is), AI-assisted content (for example proofreading with Grammarly) and a gap-filling approach (the author writes key points and AI expands them). They stress that authors remain responsible for accuracy, especially for code examples, and that quality matters more than quantity. They also mention Riverside clip generation, Gemini summaries, NotebookLM podcasts, and a predicted future business manager agent for Business Central that is not confirmed by Microsoft.

## Key points

- Readers can spot AI-generated blog content by repeated patterns (bold text, bullet points, conclusions); Gemini and ChatGPT are said to have distinct signatures such as dashes and emojis. Detection is not always reliable.
- A speaker reports reading Business Central blogs whose code did not match how Business Central works, so a professional look does not guarantee correctness.
- Three creation methods are discussed: AI-created and published directly, AI-assisted (proofreading and grammar), and AI gap-filling from the author's own key points.
- Authors should validate and verify facts and proofread AI-generated code before publishing, because they are responsible for what readers use.
- Training ChatGPT or Gemini with your own earlier writing as reference can make output closer to your personal style, but it still needs human review.
- Tools mentioned: Riverside AI clips (results are sometimes poor), Gemini audio summaries (no video yet), NotebookLM two-person podcasts from sources.
- A business manager agent for Business Central is a prediction by the speakers, not confirmed by Microsoft.
